setter hiring kit

from no setter to a scored one in four steps. this is the exact process from the last hire i ran: four candidates interviewed, one trialed, locked inside a week. nothing here is theory.

1 application form 2 triage + interview 3 paid trial week 4 scorecard from day one
1

the application form

the form's job is to shrink the pile before you spend a minute talking to anyone. every question below is a filter. paste them into a google form as-is.

the questions

  1. name, instagram handle, timezone
  2. what offers have you set for? name them. filter: can't name one → out
  3. what was your daily volume? outbound dms sent and conversations worked. the strong candidates in my last hire were doing 100+ proactive outbound a day, unprompted
  4. how many calls did you book in your best week?
  5. of the calls you booked, how many showed up? your show rate, roughly. the question every applicant skips. a real setter knows this number
  6. how many hours a day can you actually give this, and which hours in your timezone?
  7. are you setting for anyone right now?
  8. when could you start?

auto-rejects, before any interview

×no offer named in q2. "various online businesses" is no offer.
×no numbers anywhere. "loads, did really well" without a single figure.
×q5 dodged with a claim instead of a number or an honest "never tracked it".

an honest "never tracked it" is not an auto-reject. it tells you they've never been measured, which the trial fixes. a dodge tells you they will dodge your questions later too.

2

triage + interview

ten minutes per stack of applications. score each survivor out of 5 on four things, interview the top three or four, and never decide on the call.

the rubric

score /5what you're actually testing
can they settheir numbers reconcile into a funnel: dms → conversations → booked → showed → closed. ask them to break a big claim down and watch whether it adds up. vague volume talk scores a 1
will they show upproven daily volume, proactive outbound without being told. this is the gap that kills most setter hires, so weight it heaviest.
coachabletakes a correction on the call itself. offer one small pushback and watch what they do with it.
daily-dm testwould you be happy managing this person in your dms every single day. sharp questions from them count for this, silence counts against.

the three scenarios that do the real testing

run these live on the interview. the answers separate craft from talk faster than anything else you can ask.

scenario a · the quiet warm lead · always run this one "a warm lead showed real interest, then went quiet on you. walk me through exactly what you'd do to get them back."

gold: a real multi-touch cadence with escalation. change the subject, get the owner to send a voice note so the lead knows it's real, then a direct interest-check, then into a long-term follow-up list. about four touches. weak: "i'd follow up and ask if they have questions." no cadence, no timing, no escalation.

scenario b · the price question "someone opens with 'how much is it?' straight away. what do you say?"

gold: no number. reframes to tailoring, pivots into qualifying, drives to the call. weak: quotes a price or gets flustered. quoting in the dm is an instant no-hire.

scenario c · the unqualified lead "a 19 year old with no business and no money messages wanting to work with you. what do you do?"

gold: qualifies first, routes them somewhere kind, protects the calendar. weak: books everyone and lets the closer sort it out. that habit is where your no-show rate comes from, and it's the most common bad answer.

two rules for you on the call

you're also selling the seat: good setters have options. pitch the upside as more leads and a path to lead setter. and don't hand them leverage: the moment you say "good setters are hard to find," your comp negotiation is over.

3

the paid trial week

seven days, paid, in the real inbox. an interview proves they can talk about setting. the trial proves volume, show rate and follow-up craft, which no interview can.

the daily floor

50 warm outbound dms 10 dead threads revived <5 min reply time in peak hours 2 reports: start of day + end of day

week one is a ramp. you're watching bookings climb toward 3 qualified calls a day, and a strong first week lands around 12. you're judging the process, because the process is what produces the bookings.

the end-of-day report, verbatim

two messages a day in a shared channel. no forms, no dashboards to fill. they copy this, fill the blanks, send it.

start of shift🟢 on shift - [name]
end of day🔴 eod - [name] - [date]
warm outbound sent: _ / 50
old convos restarted: _ / 10
calls booked: _
close to booking: _
stuck on / need from you: _

pass or part ways

locked in if
  • daily numbers hit, consistently
  • qualifying is clean: right people booked, rest routed
  • show rate 70%+ on what they book
  • bookings trending up through the week
  • both reports in, every day
parts ways if
  • replies consistently slow
  • unqualified people on the calendar
  • warm leads dying without a follow-up
  • reports missed
  • no momentum by day five

trial comp from the real hire: 10% on low-ticket they close directly in the dms, 5% on closes from calls they booked. lock the long-term rate after the trial, and review the seat again at 90 days. one more thing: never offer the trial live on the interview call. "i'll be in touch in a day or two" keeps the trial as the prize.
